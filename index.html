Study design & approach

This is a rapid appraisal built on a non-probability, purposive sample. Findings are therefore descriptive and hypothesis-generating — they characterise how surveyed households experience and cope with extreme heat, but they are not statistically generalisable to state or national populations.

The work uses a convergence design: a household appraisal (shown in this dashboard) will be triangulated with a system-level adaptation assessment conducted through a modified-Delphi expert workshop. Together they will feed into Phase 2 of the joint climate research programme of UNICEF and the International Institute for Population Sciences (IIPS).

The assessment focuses on nutrition-sensitive dimensions of climate risk: recognition of the climate event as a threat and care-seeking behaviour; access to social safety nets, health and nutrition services; livelihood and income stability; gender dynamics in food preparation and care work; local early warning and collective coping; health/nutrition service readiness; and market functionality during climate events.

State–event mapping & the four hazards

The full framework covers four climate events, each mapped to the states where it is most salient. This dashboard currently presents the Heat arm; the other hazards are structured identically and will be enabled as their data arrives.

Sampling cascade & household selection

Within each state–event, a five-stage purposive cascade guides selection:

1District selection — at least two districts per state (one rural + one urban), ranked using IMD / SDMA / State Climate Action Plan data, or the project's District Climate Data file (heat-days, PM2.5, drought index, flood frequency).
2Site selection — two villages in the rural district and two wards in the urban district, close to a planned programme monitoring site.
3Households per site — five households per village/ward for the theme, with a minimum of ~20 households per state per theme (target 20–30).
4Household eligibility — resident >6 months; a household with a child under 2 or aged 3–5, a pregnant woman, a person with disability, or a senior (65+); exposure within the last 30 days; and informed consent.
5Diversity spread — deliberately span BPL/APL, female-headed, and SC/ST/marginalised households to maximise variation within the small sample.
Data collection & quality control

Interviews were administered digitally (KoboToolbox) by trained DRR + Nutrition field staff. The respondent was the household head or the primary caregiver of the youngest child (0–59 months). All interviews followed informed consent, and state coordinators spot-checked 1 in 5 forms for quality.

How indicators are computed here: percentages use the household as the denominator (n shown per view). Multi-select items can sum to more than 100%. “Serious heat impact” combines “Severe” and “Very severe” self-rated impact. Food/livelihood impact counts any heat effect on producing or purchasing food, excluding “No significant effect”. Some sub-questions use a reduced denominator (e.g. treatment facility is asked only of those who sought care; chronic-condition types only of those reporting a condition). Figures are computed directly from the raw survey microdata and reconcile with the study's analysis workbook.

System-level readiness (to be conducted soon) will be assessed separately via a one-day modified-Delphi “Estimate–Talk–Estimate” workshop with 12–18 experts per state across health, ICDS/WCD, agriculture, water, disaster management, urban health, IMD, civil society, academia and frontline representatives.
